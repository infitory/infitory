# System name
Fate Core

## Description
Fate Core by Evil Hat Productions (Creative Commons CC-BY 3.0). A narrative-focused RPG where characters are defined by Aspects — short descriptive phrases that can be invoked for bonuses or compelled for complications. Works with any setting. Uses Fate Dice (d6s marked +, +, blank, blank, –, –) or a d6–d6 table. The GM is the Fate Master (FM) or Game Master.

## Attributes
Note: Fate Core has no traditional attributes. Characters are defined by Skills (rated on the Ladder) and Aspects (narrative descriptors). The skill pyramid and aspects together define the character.

Skill Ladder reference:
+8 Legendary | +7 Epic | +6 Fantastic | +5 Superb | +4 Great | +3 Good | +2 Fair | +1 Average | 0 Mediocre | –1 Poor | –2 Terrible

## Derived stats
- Refresh: number of Fate Points you start each session with (default 3; reduced by Stunts)
- Stress Tracks: Physical (Physique-based) and Mental (Will-based) — each box absorbs 1 Stress hit
  - 1–2 boxes at Physique/Will 0, +1 box at 1–2, +2 boxes at 3–4, +2 boxes and extra Consequence at 5+
- Consequences: narrative wounds that absorb extra stress — Mild (−2), Moderate (−4), Severe (−6)
- Fate Points: currency for invoking Aspects and other effects; refresh each session

## Skills
Skill ratings are arranged in a Pyramid: 1 skill at peak rating, and more as you go down.
Default spread: 1 at Great (+4), 2 at Good (+3), 3 at Fair (+2), 4 at Average (+1)

Core skill list (settings can replace or modify these):
Athletics | overcome physical obstacles, dodge attacks, move quickly
Burglary | bypass security, pick locks, case a location
Contacts | know people, gather information through your network
Crafts | build, repair, and understand physical objects
Deceive | lie, mislead, and create false impressions
Drive | operate vehicles, chase and escape
Empathy | read people, sense emotions and lies
Fight | attack in close combat
Investigate | search, deduce, and piece together clues
Lore | recall knowledge, know history and arcane matters
Notice | perceive your surroundings, detect ambushes
Physique | feats of strength and endurance, resist physical harm
Provoke | intimidate, upset, and goad others into reactions
Rapport | build trust, make friends, speak in public
Resources | access to money and material goods
Shoot | attack at range
Stealth | move unseen, hide, set up ambushes
Will | mental fortitude, resist temptation and compulsion

## Dice
Roll 4 Fate Dice (4dF): each shows +1, 0, or −1 → sum them (range: −4 to +4)
Add your relevant Skill rating to the sum.
Compare to difficulty (Passive: a fixed Ladder rating) or opponent's roll (Active: their roll)

Outcomes (by margin of success, called "shifts"):
- Fail: result lower than difficulty — fail or succeed at a serious cost
- Tie: result equals difficulty — succeed at a minor cost, or partial success
- Success: 1–2 shifts — you do it cleanly
- Success with Style: 3+ shifts — you do it impressively, gain a Boost

Boosts: temporary free Aspect with one free invoke — used before the scene ends or lost

Fate Points:
- Invoke an Aspect: spend 1 FP → +2 to a roll, or reroll all dice, OR impose a –2 on opponent's roll
- Compel an Aspect: GM offers you 1 FP to let an Aspect complicate your life — accept or spend 1 FP to refuse

Creating Advantages: use a skill to create a situational Aspect (e.g. "On Fire" or "Off Balance")
→ Roll vs difficulty; on success, create Aspect with 1–2 free invokes

## Notes
### Aspects — The Core of Fate
Aspects are short phrases that describe something true about the fiction.
Every character has 5 Aspects. Situations, locations, and scenes can have Aspects too.
Types of character Aspects:
- High Concept: who you are in a phrase ("Disgraced Knight Seeking Redemption")
- Trouble: your central flaw or complication ("Everyone I Love Gets Hurt")
- Phase Aspects: from your character's backstory phases (3 more)

Aspects can be invoked (spend FP for a bonus) or compelled (GM adds a complication for a FP).
Free Invokes: earned from Create Advantage actions — no FP cost to use them.

### The Four Actions
Overcome: use a skill to get past an obstacle
Create Advantage: establish a useful Aspect on the scene or a target
Attack: try to harm another character (physical or mental)
Defend: oppose an attack or resist someone Overcoming against you

### Conflicts
Characters take turns. Exchange = one round.
Attack vs Defend: margin of success = Stress dealt to loser
Taking Stress: fill Stress boxes (up to 4) OR take a Consequence (absorbs Stress equal to its value)
Taken Out: if you can't absorb all Stress — the opponent narrates what happens to you
Concede: before rolling a defence, you can choose to lose on your own terms (gain 1 FP per Consequence absorbed)

### Stunts
Stunts are special rules that bend or break the normal rules for your character.
Common Stunt patterns:
- "+2 to [skill] when [specific circumstance]"
- "Use [skill] instead of [other skill] in [situation]"
- "Once per session, [do something unusual]"
Each Stunt beyond the first 3 reduces your Refresh by 1. Minimum Refresh: 1.

### Fate Points & Refresh
Refresh = your Fate Point floor at session start.
During play: earn FP by accepting Compels; spend FP to Invoke Aspects.
At session end: reset to Refresh (if you have fewer) but keep extras if you have more.

### Extras (Optional)
Extras are rules for powers, gear, organisations, and other special elements beyond the core rules.
Use them to model magic systems, special weapons, or setting-specific mechanics.
Extras are defined collaboratively by GM and players.
